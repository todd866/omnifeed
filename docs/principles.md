# Ground rules

Set by Ian on 20 September 2026.

## FOSS throughout

Publish our application, collectors, firmware, protocol, hardware design sources, bill of materials, build instructions and useful research as work progresses. Prefer editable sources and reproducible builds over exports alone. Use free/open-source implementation dependencies and document licences. A public repository without a licence is insufficient.

The initial original documentation is MIT licensed. Revisit software copyleft and an appropriate open-hardware licence before accepting outside contributions or publishing derived PCB designs; preserve all upstream obligations. Do not relicense third-party work as ours.

Self-hosting must be a supported architectural path. Vercel/R2 are optional deployment targets, not required proprietary parts of the core. Keep storage and hosting behind portable interfaces; document a fully self-hosted route when implementation begins. Avoid mandatory proprietary AI services; any AI enhancement needs an optional interface and a FOSS/local path.

The existing Bose electronics are proprietary legacy hardware being adapted. Audit candidate modules, radio firmware and SDKs for closed components before selection; record limitations explicitly rather than labelling the whole physical device fully open. Our additions and published designs should be open; unresolved binary requirements are a selection constraint.

## Publish engineering, keep personal data private

Public by default: original code, design files, experiments, findings, synthetic fixtures and documentation. Keep private media libraries, account sessions, credentials and personal source material outside the repository and public releases. Source links and small purpose-built fixtures can document an integration without republishing an account’s contents.

## Explore the full scope of media handling

Investigate ambitious personal collection, indexing, caching, format shifting, accessibility, interoperability and organisation. Do not collapse copyright law, platform terms, API availability, DRM and engineering feasibility into one restriction.

Maintain an operation-by-operation evidence record: source and access basis; copying or transformation performed; personal versus public output; applicable jurisdiction; relevant exceptions or constraints; and unresolved questions. “Fair use” is a research objective, not a blanket legal conclusion. Determine which jurisdiction-specific concepts apply before making claims.

Prefer concrete supported limits to speculative warnings. Do not assume a subscription creates a portable audio file, or that an API’s missing feature settles what other lawful methods are possible. Evaluate methods on their actual facts.
